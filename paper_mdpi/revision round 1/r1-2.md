# Response to Reviewer 1, Comment 2

> **Comment.** The proof must be cryptographically linked to the authorization context. The current proof statement is not linked to the requester identity, protected resource, or session context. As mentioned in the manuscript, single-use of the nonce does not allow reuse of the same session parameters, but it does not prevent replay of a valid proof captured by an active party using fresh parameters. This is a significant limitation of the authorization guarantees of the current prototype. **The authors would ideally add the relevant requester/resource/session values to the proved statement and experimentally evaluate the resulting circuit/public-input and gas cost changes.** If this cannot be implemented in the current version, the security claims and deployment implications should be made even more explicit throughout the manuscript, including the abstract and conclusion.

**Response.** We thank the reviewer for identifying this authorization-context gap. We agree and have implemented the proposed binding in both the circuit and the on-chain access-control path.

In the submitted system, the public statement contained the authorization output, policy commitment, and parameter commitment, but no value derived from the requester, resource, or session. The contract checked nonce reuse only from the transaction parameters. Consequently, a captured proof could be paired with a fresh nonce or another resource while the proof itself remained unchanged.

For the revised construction, let

```text
χ = (id_req, id_res, ν)
d_χ = Poseidon(id_req || id_res || ν),
```

where `ν` is the session nonce. The circuit takes the three components of `χ` as private circuit inputs and enforces the hard equality `d_χ = Poseidon(χ)`, with `d_χ` exposed as a public input. The ACM receives the same three values as transaction parameters, recomputes the digest on-chain, and requires it to equal the proof's public signal before verification. It also checks the resource–policy association, the public request epoch, and nonce single-use for `(policy, resource, ν, id_req)`. Thus, within one ACM deployment, changing the requester identifier, resource, or nonce causes rejection rather than permitting a captured proof to be replayed with fresh parameters.

The three context values are private with respect to the Groth16 relation but are not hidden from the blockchain: they are present in transaction calldata so that the ACM can recompute the digest. We therefore describe `id_req` as a requester identifier or pseudonym, not as a hidden or universally authenticated real-world identity. The current RC1 statement contains five signals:

```text
(isAuthorized, h, pp, d_χ, τ),
```

namely one output and four public inputs; `τ` is a separate request-epoch input added for temporal policy checks.

We measured the context-binding change in isolation against the length-bound circuit immediately preceding it. It adds 605 R1CS constraints, increasing the total from 75,185 to 75,790 (0.8%), and adds one public input. Verifier gas increases from 228,754 to 235,914, a difference of 7,160 gas (3.1%). The on-chain Poseidon(3) recomputation adds 49,298 gas. These are marginal measurements for this change; they should not be obtained by subtracting the final system totals because subsequent, independent revisions also changed the circuit and access record. The frozen RC1 circuit, including those later revisions, has 81,740 constraints and four public inputs plus one output. The 1,773 Polygon Amoy executions used for the revised evaluation were generated with this RC1 circuit and its regenerated proving and verification artifacts.

The revised implementation is covered at both layers. At the circuit layer, the DUO V3 tamper test rejects a context digest computed for another nonce. At the contract layer, the tests confirm agreement between the circuit-side and EVM Poseidon implementations and reject substitutions of the requester, resource, and nonce. The end-to-end suite reuses one valid proof as a negative control and confirms that a fresh nonce or another resource governed by the same policy fails with a context-binding mismatch. All targeted tests pass, and the RC1 manifest, verifier ABI, and cryptographic artifacts validate successfully.

We have kept the claim precise: the change closes the reviewer's fresh-parameter replay within one ACM deployment. The digest does not currently include the transaction submitter or a chain/contract domain, so the revised Discussion identifies exact-context front-running and cross-deployment reuse as separate hardening considerations.

The manuscript has been revised as follows:

- **Design, “Commitment Binding and Proof Generation.”** We define the context digest and the five-signal statement, explain the circuit and ACM checks, and remove the former future-work language.
- **Problem and Threat Model.** We distinguish a requester pseudonym from real-world identity and attribute-origin authentication.
- **Discussion.** We report the measured marginal cost and state the scope of the resulting guarantee.
- **Evaluation cost profile.** We report the final RC1 circuit and public interface together with the revised Polygon Amoy gas, calldata, and prover measurements.
