\begin{reviewcomment}{2}
	The current proof statement leaves important soundness gaps.
	The paper acknowledges that the program length is not bound into the policy commitment and that the proof is not bound to the requester, resource, or session context.
	These are not minor implementation details: they directly affect the correctness and replay-resistance of the authorization workflow.
	These issues should be fixed in the design and experimentally re-evaluated, rather than mainly left to future work.
\end{reviewcomment}

\begin{reviewresponse}{2}
	Both gaps are closed in the revised design and implementation, and the evaluation was repeated on the revised system.

	The policy commitment now covers the program length, $h = \mathsf{Poseidon}(\mathsf{H}_{\mathrm{pol}}(P_{\mathrm{enc}}) \parallel \ell \parallel r)$, and the same private $\ell$ drives both the commitment check and the interpreter, so an authorizing proof cannot rest on a shorter stack-consistent prefix.
	The proof statement now carries a public context digest $d_{\chi} = \mathsf{Poseidon}(\mathit{id}_{\mathrm{req}} \parallel \mathit{id}_{\mathrm{res}} \parallel \nu)$ that the circuit constrains and the access-control contract recomputes from the transaction parameters, so a captured proof cannot be paired with a fresh nonce, another resource, or another requester within a deployment.
	The measured marginal costs (88 constraints for the length binding; 605 constraints, one public input, and about $56{,}000$ gas for the context binding including the on-chain Poseidon recomputation) are given in the responses to Reviewer~1, Comments~1 and~2, which also describe the regression and end-to-end tests.

	The re-evaluation is complete rather than partial.
	All of-record numbers in the revised manuscript come from a new capture of $1{,}077$ policies and $1{,}773$ Polygon Amoy executions under the frozen revised release (five-signal statement, length- and context-bound relation, credential-derived profile attributes), followed by a new un-padded ablation run of $1{,}119$ executions on the same policy subset.
	The cost profile reflects the revised circuit ($81{,}740$ constraints) and the revised on-chain path, and the results sections report the outcome of the privacy attacks on the revised transcript, including two criterion failures that we report and trace to the padding's random draws rather than to the policy (Reviewer~1, Comments~5 and~6).

	\RevisionLocation{Section ``TPS-ZKPI Design,'' subsection ``Commitment Binding and Proof Generation,'' policy-length binding.}
	\begin{revisiontext}
		To bind each proof to a provider-defined hidden policy, the provider samples commitment randomness $r$ uniformly from the scalar field and computes
		\begin{align*}
			q & = \mathsf{H}_{\mathrm{pol}}(P_{\mathrm{enc}}), \\
			h & = \mathsf{Com}(P_{\mathrm{enc}},\ell; r)
			= \mathsf{Poseidon}(q\parallel \ell\parallel r),
		\end{align*}
		where $q$ is the Poseidon-based digest of the fixed-length encoded policy, $\ell$ is the private program length, $r$ is the commitment randomness, and $h$ is the policy commitment stored on-chain.
		The semantics of an encoded policy are determined by the pair $(P_{\mathrm{enc}},\ell)$, since $\ell$ identifies the active prefix of the padded token array.
		Accordingly, the circuit supplies the same private value $\ell$ to both the commitment verifier and the policy interpreter.
		Under the collision resistance of Poseidon, an accepting proof bound to $h$ cannot substitute a different program length and therefore cannot evaluate an alternative stack-consistent prefix of $P_{\mathrm{enc}}$.
	\end{revisiontext}

	\RevisionLocation{Section ``TPS-ZKPI Design,'' subsection ``Commitment Binding and Proof Generation,'' authorization-context binding.}
	\begin{revisiontext}
		For an access request, let
		\begin{equation*}
			\chi=(\mathit{id}_{\mathrm{req}},\mathit{id}_{\mathrm{res}},\nu),
			\qquad
			d_{\chi}=\mathsf{Poseidon}(\mathit{id}_{\mathrm{req}}\parallel
			\mathit{id}_{\mathrm{res}}\parallel\nu),
		\end{equation*}
		where $\nu$ is a fresh session nonce.
		The context tuple $\chi$ consists of private circuit inputs, while its digest $d_{\chi}$ is public.
		The current public-signal vector is
		\begin{equation*}
			(1,h,\mathit{pp},d_{\chi},\tau),
		\end{equation*}
		where $\mathit{pp}$ is a fixed-format commitment to the per-predicate parameters and $\tau$ is the request epoch used by the temporal checks.
		Although the three components of $\chi$ are not separate Groth16 public inputs, the ACM receives them as transaction parameters in order to recompute $d_{\chi}$; they are therefore visible in transaction calldata.
		The proof certifies the conjunction that $C(P_{\mathrm{enc}},\ell,(a,b))=1$, $h=\mathsf{Com}(P_{\mathrm{enc}},\ell;r)$, $d_{\chi}=\mathsf{Poseidon}(\chi)$, and the selected external-predicate witnesses satisfy their policy-bound verification relations.
		Before verifying $\pi$, the ACM checks that the resource is governed by the stated policy, that $\tau$ is current, that the nonce has not been used for the same requester--resource context, and that the digest recomputed from the transaction parameters equals the public signal $d_{\chi}$.
		Thus, within one ACM deployment, a proof generated for one requester identifier, resource, and nonce cannot be accepted under a different tuple.
	\end{revisiontext}
