\subsection{Cost and Performance}
\label{sec:eval-performance}

At $L=64$ and eight Merkle levels, the prototype has $81{,}980$ constraints and mean gas costs of $219{,}757$ for commit and $723{,}565$ for access (Table~\ref{tab:cost-profile}).
The gas varies only within narrow ranges, from $219{,}688$ to $237{,}664$ for commit and from $723{,}446$ to $741{,}458$ for access.
Subtracting the base cost of $21{,}000$ and the calldata cost from each transaction (Section~\ref{subsec:transcript-normalization}) leaves an execution cost of $196{,}964$ gas for every commit, $70{,}400$ for every resource registration, and $695{,}254$ and $695{,}264$ for every denied and granted access, respectively, apart from $17{,}940$ additional gas on the first transaction of each sending account.
Across the $1{,}977$ Amoy executions on the two-vCPU capture server, which ran four capture processes concurrently, mean witness and proving times are $1.140$ and $3.990$~s.

\input{tables/tab_cost_scaling.tex}
To assess how circuit capacity affects proving costs, we generate $30$ verified proofs per configuration using rapidsnark on an Apple M1 (Table~\ref{tab:cost-scaling}).
Increasing program capacity $L$ from $32$ to $128$ raises the constraint count from $64{,}945$ to $134{,}009$ and median proving time from $0.411$ to $0.734$~s.
At $L=64$, increasing the number of Merkle levels from $4$ to $16$ raises the constraint count from $73{,}876$ to $97{,}468$, whereas median proving time rises only from $0.512$ to $0.571$~s.

Within the fixed circuit ($L=64$, eight Merkle levels), median witness and proving times vary little across the evaluated policies, ranging from $0.335$ to $0.354$~s and from $0.484$ to $0.545$~s, respectively (Table~\ref{tab:workload-scaling}).
For profile-backed policies, the harness adds about $0.64$~s by issuing a fresh credential per request, bringing median end-to-end time to approximately $1.47$--$1.48$~s.
Pre-issued credentials can be reused in deployment.

With $100$ requests per concurrency level, offline throughput stays between $0.79$ and $0.94$ proofs/s as concurrency increases from one to four, while median latency rises from $1.2$ to $4.1$~s (Table~\ref{tab:workload-scaling}).
These measurements include the harness's evidence preparation and credential issuance; latency excludes queueing.
