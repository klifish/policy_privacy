\abstract{In blockchain-based healthcare data sharing, consent policies are expected to be enforced in a verifiable and auditable manner. However, public blockchain transparency may expose sensitive policy information through on-chain representations, verification interfaces, and observable execution artifacts. Existing privacy-preserving solutions mainly protect data contents, user identities, or transaction details, but they insufficiently address the confidentiality of governing consent policies and policy-dependent leakage during enforcement. To address this gap, we propose the Trusted Proof Service-assisted Zero-Knowledge Policy Interpreter (TPS-ZKPI), which enables authorization decisions to be verified while limiting leakage about policy content, policy identity, and policy structure and parameters. Policy confidentiality is conditional on an explicit off-chain trust boundary: the TPS must keep plaintext policy information and requester authorization evidence secret. To reduce policy-dependent leakage through public verification artifacts, TPS-ZKPI combines fixed-length policy encoding, a reusable zero-knowledge policy interpreter, commitment-based policy binding, and public-chain transcript normalization. We evaluate TPS-ZKPI against metadata-only adversaries that observe public-chain artifacts and conduct inference attacks on these three leakage targets. The results show that under a multi-seed permutation-test criterion, no modeled supervised or unsupervised attack recovers hidden policy information above the random guessing baseline, and a paired gas-padding ablation attributes this protection specifically to gas-transcript normalization at a $38$--$40\%$ gas overhead.}
