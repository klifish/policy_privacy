\abstract{
	Blockchain-based consent management makes healthcare data sharing auditable, but on a public blockchain, enforcement can reveal the consent policies.
	These policies encode conditions such as disease-specific research restrictions, whose exposure can disclose sensitive information about the data subject.
	To address this, we propose the Trusted Proof Service (TPS)-assisted Zero-Knowledge Policy Interpreter (TPS-ZKPI), in which the TPS generates zero-knowledge proofs of compliance that a smart contract verifies against committed policies.
	In TPS-ZKPI, we compile each policy into a fixed-length program for a reusable zero-knowledge policy interpreter, so all policies share one verification interface, and mask residual gas differences with randomized gas padding.
	We evaluate TPS-ZKPI on Polygon Amoy with supervised and unsupervised attacks in which a passive public-chain observer tries to infer hidden policy properties from public-chain metadata.
	The results show that $43$ of $45$ supervised tasks stay below our leakage criterion, and both failures trace to chance correlations with the padding.
	Furthermore, an ablation shows that without padding the same attacks often recover policy content and structure, while padding adds about $46\%$ gas to access and $38\%$ to policy commitment.
	Overall, this paper shows empirically that consent enforcement on a public blockchain can resist the modeled metadata attacks, provided the TPS is trusted.
}
