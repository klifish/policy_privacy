\abstract{
	Blockchain-based consent management makes healthcare data sharing auditable, but public-chain enforcement can reveal the consent policies.
	Their conditions, such as disease-specific research restrictions, can disclose sensitive information about the data subject.
	To address this, we propose the Trusted Proof Service (TPS)-assisted Zero-Knowledge Policy Interpreter (TPS-ZKPI), in which the TPS generates zero-knowledge proofs of access decisions that a smart contract verifies against committed policies.
	We compile each policy into a fixed-length program for a reusable zero-knowledge policy interpreter, so all policies share one verification interface and a fixed-format public-chain transcript.
	We evaluate TPS-ZKPI on Polygon Amoy with supervised and unsupervised attacks by a passive observer inferring hidden policy properties from public-chain metadata.
	The results show no leakage in any of the $44$ supervised tasks under a criterion requiring mean AUC above $0.55$ and significance in at least half of $30$ splits, and unsupervised clustering recovers no policy structure beyond chance level.
	Overall, our empirical results show that TPS-ZKPI resists the modeled metadata attacks and provides auditable consent enforcement on a public blockchain.
}
