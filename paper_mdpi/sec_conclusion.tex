\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented TPS-ZKPI, a Trusted Proof Service-assisted zero-knowledge approach to policy-private consent enforcement on public blockchains.
In TPS-ZKPI, the data provider commits to a hidden consent policy on-chain, the TPS generates a zero-knowledge proof of each access decision under this policy, and a smart contract verifies the proof.
Each policy is compiled into a fixed-length program for a reusable zero-knowledge policy interpreter, so all policies share one verification interface, and randomized gas padding masks the remaining gas differences.
We evaluated TPS-ZKPI on the Polygon Amoy testnet with supervised and unsupervised attacks in which a passive public-chain observer tried to infer hidden policy properties from public-chain metadata.
The results showed that only two of the 45 supervised tasks met our leakage criterion, that both failures traced to chance correlations with the padding, and that unsupervised clustering found no policy structure beyond the same padding effect.
Furthermore, the gas-padding ablation showed that, without padding, the same attacks often recovered policy content and structure, which confirmed that padding protects the gas channel at a fixed gas cost per transaction.
Overall, this paper showed empirically that TPS-ZKPI enables auditable consent enforcement on a public blockchain that resists the modeled metadata attacks.
