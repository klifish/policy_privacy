\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented TPS-ZKPI, a Trusted Proof Service-assisted zero-knowledge approach to policy-private consent enforcement on public blockchains.
In TPS-ZKPI, the data provider commits to a hidden consent policy on-chain, the TPS generates a zero-knowledge proof of each access decision under this policy, and a smart contract verifies the proof.
Each policy is compiled into a fixed-length program for a reusable zero-knowledge policy interpreter, so all policies share one verification interface and a fixed-format public-chain transcript.
We evaluated TPS-ZKPI on the Polygon Amoy testnet with supervised and unsupervised attacks in which a passive public-chain observer tried to infer hidden policy properties from public-chain metadata.
The results showed no leakage under our criterion in 43 of the 45 supervised tasks; the other two traced to chance correlations with the padding, and unsupervised clustering found no policy structure beyond the same padding effect.
Overall, this paper showed empirically that TPS-ZKPI resists the modeled metadata attacks and provides auditable consent enforcement on a public blockchain.
