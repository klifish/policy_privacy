\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper studied policy-private consent enforcement in blockchain-based
healthcare data sharing under public-chain observability and an explicit TPS trust assumption.
We proposed TPS-ZKPI, a Trusted Proof Service-assisted zero-knowledge approach that binds hidden provider-defined policies to on-chain commitments, evaluates Boolean-based consent policies through a reusable policy interpreter, and normalizes public verification artifacts to reduce metadata-level leakage.
Across supervised and unsupervised attacks on PCH, PI, and SPP, no modeled adversary recovered hidden policy information reproducibly above chance under our multi-seed leakage criterion, and a paired gas-padding ablation attributed the gas-channel protection specifically to randomized gas padding---at a gas overhead of about $38\%$ on access and $40\%$ on commit.

These results hold for a metadata-only public-chain adversary, and the privacy
claim is empirical. The remaining limitations and future directions discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion} include stronger adaptive adversaries, off-chain leakage, richer policy languages, reduced-trust proof generation, and privacy-preserving resource discovery.
