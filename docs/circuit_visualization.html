🔄 Circuit Data Flow

1
Private Witness Input Processing
The circuit receives private inputs: policy P (RPN tokens), requester attributes a, external predicate inputs, and commitment randomizer r. These form the complete witness w = (P, a, p, r).
2
Policy Commitment Verification
PolicyCommitmentVerifier checks that Com(P;r) = h using Poseidon hash. This ensures the committed policy matches the one being evaluated, preserving policy privacy while enabling verification.
3
External Predicate Verification
ExternalPredicateVerifiers (one per predicate) verify Merkle proofs, credential signatures, range proofs, and ontology-linked terms. Each produces a predicate value p_j ∈ {0,1}.
4
Policy Evaluation
PostfixPolicyInterpreter evaluates the policy P against requester attributes a and verified predicates p, computing C(P,(a,p)). Uses RPN evaluation with stack-based computation.
5
Constraint Enforcement
Boolean constraints ensure requester attributes are binary. Type constraints validate predicate types are in valid range {0,1,2,3}. These maintain circuit integrity.
6
Final Authorization
AND gate combines commitment validity and policy evaluation result. Authorization requires both: valid commitment AND satisfied policy. Output isAuthorized indicates final access decision.

🔬 Technical Implementation

Cryptographic Primitives:

  • Poseidon Hash: Used for policy commitments and Merkle trees
  • Groth16 Proof System: Provides succinctness and fast verification
  • BN254 Elliptic Curve: Underlying cryptographic foundation

Privacy Guarantees:

  • Policy Privacy: Actual policy rules never revealed on-chain
  • Attribute Privacy: Individual attributes remain private
  • Zero-Knowledge: Only policy satisfaction is proven

Performance Characteristics:

  • Constraints: ~500k R1CS constraints
  • Proof Generation: 4-5 seconds on modern hardware
  • Verification: ~10ms on-chain with gas cost ~180k
  • Trusted Setup: One-time ceremony for circuit parameters
