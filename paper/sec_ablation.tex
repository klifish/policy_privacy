\subsection{Ablation: gas-normalization positive control}
\label{sec:eval-ablation}

The main results show that the full mechanism hides content, policy identity, and
structure/parameters. The ablation asks \emph{why}: how much of the residual attack
surface is removed by randomized access-gas padding after the fixed circuit and
commitment-only interface have already eliminated calldata, proof-size, and event-log
channels.

We compare two aligned protection profiles on the same selected policy set.
\textbf{B1} uses the fixed circuit, commitment-only interface, and TPS execution profile
without commit/access gas padding. \textbf{B2} is the full mechanism used in the main
evaluation, with randomized access-gas padding. This is the positive control for the
gas channel: calldata, proof size, and event-log shape are already fixed, so B1 is an
un-padded gas-transcript control rather than a run with every normalization layer
removed. The same features, splits, and classifiers produce well-above-chance AUCs on
B1 when padding is absent, while B2 returns the attacker toward chance. Thus the main
privacy claim rests on a real ablation contrast rather than on a synthetic label
derived from an input feature. The paired leakage metric is
%
\[
  \Delta_{\text{leak}} =
  |\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{B1}} - 0.5| -
  |\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{B2}} - 0.5|.
\]
%
Positive values mean padding moved the attacker closer to chance, and we bound each
$\Delta_{\text{leak}}$ with a $500$-iteration bootstrap $95\%$ confidence interval.
Table~\ref{tab:ablation-gas-padding} is intentionally separated from the main privacy
verdicts: the main verdicts answer whether the full mechanism is private; this ablation
attributes that privacy to a specific defense layer and reports its gas cost.

\paragraph{Padding removes otherwise-recoverable signal.} With padding disabled, attacks
that are non-informative on the deployed system become measurably effective. The
random-forest distinguisher on B1 reaches AUC up to $0.81$ on SPP parameter-magnitude
contrasts (\texttt{paramType none/small}) and $0.76$ on structural contrasts
(\texttt{and\_only/negation\_heavy}), while the corresponding B2 AUCs fall back to
$0.41$--$0.47$; the bootstrap $\Delta_{\text{leak}}$ is positive with a $95\%$ CI that
excludes zero. The clustering attacker shows the same pattern: PI pairwise clustering
reaches debiased significance on $68\%$ of pairs under B1 (mean ARI $0.50$) versus $5\%$
under B2 (mean ARI $0.0015$), and SPP logic-structure clustering drops from ARI $0.067$
(B1) to $-0.006$ (B2). This establishes randomized access-gas padding as the defense
layer responsible for the gas-channel protection reported in
Sections~\ref{sec:eval-pch}--\ref{sec:eval-clustering}, rather than weak attacks or
insufficient sample size.

\paragraph{Cost.} Padding is not free: it raises mean commit gas from $216{,}562$ (B1)
to $303{,}556$ (B2), a $40.2\%$ increase, and mean access gas from $546{,}876$ to
$753{,}310$, a $37.7\%$ increase. This overhead is the principal efficiency tradeoff of
the design. Table~\ref{tab:cost-profile} adds a single-point DEPTH=20 cost
characterization. Gas, logs, and transaction objects are taken from the Polygon Amoy
executed datasets; transaction hashes are stored in the B1/B2 executed datasets. The
captured transaction \texttt{dataLength} values are reported instead of
\texttt{accessCalldataLength}, which is null in these artifacts. Proving time is local
prover timing from the B1 run, not an on-chain measurement. Cost-vs-DEPTH scaling is
left to future work because only DEPTH=20 is compiled and evaluated here.

\paragraph{Scope.} B2 reuses the existing deployed Polygon Amoy run, so B1 and B2 are
non-contemporaneous; \texttt{blockDifference} is excluded from every attack model to
avoid confounding the comparison with timing rather than policy-dependent structure.
