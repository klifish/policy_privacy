\subsection{Structure \& Parameter Privacy (SPP)}
\label{sec:eval-spp}

\paragraph{Property.} SPP requires that neither the \emph{shape} of a policy (logical
structure, nesting depth, predicate coverage) nor the \emph{magnitude} of its parameters
(whitelist/set sizes) leaks to the observer. The supervised attack organizes the test
along three annotation axes---logical structure type, predicate count, and parameter
magnitude---and attacks each pair of buckets within an axis. \texttt{structureType} has
five buckets: \texttt{trivial} (a single predicate with no boolean connective, e.g.\
policy logic \texttt{"GR"}), \texttt{and\_only}, \texttt{or\_only}, \texttt{and\_or\_mixed},
and \texttt{negation\_heavy}.

\paragraph{Supervised attack.} Table~\ref{tab:spp-contrasts} reports all 22 pairwise
contrasts over 30 policy-disjoint seeds across \texttt{structureType},
\texttt{predicateCombo}, and \texttt{paramType}. AUC is the higher-scoring of logistic
regression and random forest per contrast, since either can be the stronger attacker
depending on the pair and both are checked for gating.
Every contrast has $\mathrm{AUC}_{\text{mean}}\in[0.421,0.530]$ and a significant-seed
fraction at or below $20\%$, so all 22 pass and SPP passes overall. We gate on AUC and
the permutation test. Single-split accuracy-style diagnostics can run large on imbalanced
contrasts, but this is the majority-class artifact described in
Section~\ref{sec:eval-method}---a near-constant predictor scoring apparent gains
without genuine separation---which the AUC-plus-permutation criterion correctly reads
as chance. Section~\ref{sec:eval-ablation} provides the corresponding positive control:
when gas padding is removed, the same SPP attacker recovers structural and
parameter-magnitude signal, and that signal collapses under the full normalization.
