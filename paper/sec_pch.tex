\subsection{Policy Content Hiding (PCH)}
\label{sec:eval-pch}

\paragraph{Property} PCH requires that the on-chain observer cannot recover the \emph{content} of a policy---its DUO data-use terms or the ICD-10 disease codes it references---better than chance.

\paragraph{Scope of the labels} We restrict the generated policies to labels with enough support for a statistically powered one-vs-rest attack: six DUO data-use terms (GR, DS, RS, POA, GS, CC; smallest class $n{=}30$) and disease codes aggregated to the ten ICD-10 chapters. 
The complete DUO has 23 data-use terms and ICD-10 has tens of thousands of leaf codes; generating and executing enough policies for every term to reach adequate statistical power would inflate the experiment beyond what is feasible on-chain.
This category-level granularity is also the right one for the privacy question---the observer cares whether the \emph{category} of permitted use or disease leaks, not which leaf code is used---and it is a conservative choice: attacks on coarser, better-supported labels are \emph{easier} than on rare leaf terms, so passing here does not understate the leak. 
The label restriction also cannot favor the defenses: the commitment-only interface and gas padding are content-agnostic, behaving identically whichever terms a policy carries.

\paragraph{Supervised attack} Each of the six DUO terms (GR, DS, RS, POA, GS, CC) defines a one-vs-rest binary recovery task, on which we run the multi-seed pipeline of Section~\ref{sec:eval-method}.
Table~\ref{tab:pch-terms} reports the per-term verdicts: every DUO task has $\mathrm{AUC}_{\text{mean}}\in[0.490,0.527]$ and a significant-seed fraction of at most $13\%$, so none approaches either leg of the failure criterion.
The same pipeline applied to the ten ICD-10 chapters (one-vs-rest over the ICD-bearing rows only, median test $n{=}300$ per chapter; Table~\ref{tab:pch-icd10}) passes all ten.
The single highest AUC is ch6's $\mathrm{AUC}_{\text{mean}}=0.573\pm0.061$ with $17\%$ significant seeds; it clears the AUC leg ($0.573>0.55$) but not the reproducibility leg ($17\%<50\%$), so it is a boundary case rather than a stable channel. We report such cases rather than suppress them.
