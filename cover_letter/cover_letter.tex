\textbf{Significance and context.}
Blockchain-based consent management promises verifiable and auditable
enforcement of healthcare data-sharing policies, but public-chain transparency
creates a privacy problem that existing work leaves open: the consent policies
themselves. Prior privacy-preserving blockchain solutions protect data
contents, user identities, or transaction details, and even hidden-policy
encryption schemes do not control the policy-dependent artifacts---calldata
layout, events, verifier interfaces, and gas usage---that a public chain
exposes while enforcing a policy. This matters in healthcare, where a consent
policy built from biomedical data-use terms (e.g., Data Use Ontology
conditions) can itself reveal sensitive information about the data subject and
the protected resource.

Our manuscript addresses this gap with TPS-ZKPI, a Trusted Proof
Service-assisted zero-knowledge policy interpreter. It compiles heterogeneous
Boolean consent policies into fixed-length programs evaluated by a single
reusable zero-knowledge circuit, binds them to on-chain commitments, and
normalizes the public-chain transcript so that verification reveals minimal
policy-dependent information. Policy confidentiality rests on an explicit,
delimited off-chain trust boundary. Beyond the construction, the manuscript contributes an
attack-driven empirical evaluation on the Polygon Amoy testnet: supervised and
unsupervised metadata-only attacks target policy content, policy identity, and
policy structure and parameters under a multi-seed permutation-test criterion.
No modeled attack recovers hidden policy information reproducibly above
chance, and a paired gas-padding ablation isolates randomized gas padding as
the defense that closes the gas channel, at a measured overhead of about
38\% on access and 40\% on commit. To our knowledge, this is the first work to
define and empirically test policy privacy for blockchain consent enforcement
at the level of the public-chain transcript rather than the policy
representation alone.

\textbf{Fit to the journal.}
The manuscript falls squarely within the scope of \emph{Blockchains}: it
concerns privacy and security of smart-contract-based systems, zero-knowledge
proofs on public blockchains, and a concrete healthcare data-sharing
application, combining protocol design with a reproducible on-chain empirical
evaluation.
